# Clear writing

The reader has only the page, and less time than the writer thinks.

## Before writing or editing

Know three things. Ask if they are not clear from the request.

1. **Who reads it**, and what they already know.
2. **What they should know or do** after reading.
3. **The tone** the situation calls for.

## Principles

- **Put the point first.** The conclusion, request or answer goes in the first two sentences. Background comes after, if at all.
- **One idea per sentence, one topic per paragraph.**
- **Use the specific word.** "The server ran out of memory at 14:10" beats "there was an issue". Give numbers, names and dates.
- **Prefer verbs.** "We decided" beats "a decision was made"; "to analyse" beats "to perform an analysis of".
- **Cut what carries nothing**: "it is important to note that", "in order to", "basically", "very", "I just wanted to". If a sentence can go without loss, it goes.
- **Name who does what.** Passive voice is fine only when the actor does not matter.
- **Keep one term for one thing.** Changing the word makes the reader think the thing changed.
- **Say it plainly.** No clichés, no hype, no decoration.
- **A request names the action, the person and the deadline.**

## When editing someone's text

- Keep their voice, meaning and language. Change what hinders the reader, not what you would have phrased differently.
- Keep every fact, number and name unchanged. If something looks wrong, flag it; do not correct it silently.
- Match the edit to the request: "proofread" means errors only, "tighten" means shorter with the same content, "rewrite" gives you freedom.
- Do not make it longer unless something needed is missing.

## Formats

- **Email:** a subject line that states the point. The request or news first. Short paragraphs. One email, one topic.
- **Chat message:** the full question in one message, with what you already tried.
- **Report or documentation:** a summary at the top that can stand alone, headings that say what the section concludes, lists and tables only for content that is a list or a table.

## Answer format

Give the finished text first, ready to copy. Below it, at most three lines on what you changed and why, and any facts you could not verify or points that remain unclear. Do not explain every edit.
