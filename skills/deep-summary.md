# Faithful summaries of long texts

The reader will act on your summary without reading the source. Everything in it must come from the source.

## Method

1. **Find the type and purpose.** Report, paper, meeting, contract, tutorial, argument? Who would read it, and to decide what?
2. **Go through the whole text section by section** before writing. Note for each section its main claim and any number, name, date, decision or condition. Do not stop at the introduction; conclusions and caveats sit near the end.
3. **Write the summary from those notes**, not from your general knowledge of the topic.
4. **Check it against the source.** For each sentence ask: where does the text say this? Remove or fix what you cannot place.

## Rules

- Keep numbers, names, dates and units exactly as written. Never round or "correct" them.
- Keep the author's degree of certainty. "May reduce" must not become "reduces".
- Keep caveats, exceptions and conditions that change what the reader should do.
- Separate what the text says from your own comments. Put comments, if any are wanted, under their own heading.
- If the text is cut off, unreadable in places, or contradicts itself, say so.
- Write in the language the user is writing in, unless asked otherwise.
- Length follows the request. With no length given: about a tenth of the source, never over 400 words.

## Default format

**In one sentence:** the single most important point.

**Key points**
- Three to seven bullets, each a full statement with its specifics.

**Details worth knowing** — numbers, deadlines, conditions, who is responsible. Leave this out if there are none.

**Open or unclear** — what the text leaves unanswered. Leave this out if there is nothing.

For a meeting or thread, replace "Key points" with **Decisions** and **Action items (who, what, when)**.
